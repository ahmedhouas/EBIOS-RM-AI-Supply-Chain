## Executive summary

The team aligned on a five-workshop EBIOS-RM project producing a risk-analysis report, followed by updates/reduction of the Information System Security Policy (ISSP) and Business Continuity Plan (BCP). The overall schedule discussed was approximately four months. Workshop 1 is the immediate priority because it defines the study scope, asset inventory, and security baseline needed by later work. The team agreed to start with a small, focused group of roughly 3–4 contributors for Workshop 1 and decide Workshop 2 staffing after reviewing Workshop 1 results. Several role assignments and exact later deadlines remain provisional.



## Decisions and alignment

- **Project outputs:** Workshop outputs feed a consolidated risk-analysis report; the ISSP and BCP are updated after the workshops.
- **Project organization:** Maintain project planning, team organization, and meeting reports alongside preparation materials, workshop outputs, drafts/reviews, and final deliverables.
- **Workshop 1 sequence:** Introduce the organization/use case and system architecture; define the study scope and inventories; define CIA classification criteria and classify assets; then identify feared events. This resolves the discussion about whether scope or classification should come first: scope and inventory precede classification, followed by feared events.
- **Workshop staffing:** Workshop 1 receives about 3–4 contributors because of its workload and its importance to Workshop 2. Workshop 2 staffing will be decided after the Workshop 1 results are reviewed. A broader proposal was for contributors to participate in more than one workshop, with the exact distribution still to be finalized.
- **Documentation platform:** GitHub is the current proposal for versioned project documents. Use individual branches and careful commits; avoid force-pushing or overwriting others’ work.
- **Meeting cadence:** Keep a fixed Friday meeting and arrange additional discussions only as needed; use email or group chat for routine coordination where a meeting is unnecessary.



## Project scope and deliverable structure

The case is a supply chain with AI and workflows in scope. The project should focus on the critical parts of the case study. The intended deliverables are:

1. EBIOS-RM outputs from five workshops.
2. A consolidated risk-analysis report, reviewed and validated before submission to the professor.
3. Updated/reduced ISSP.
4. Updated/reduced BCP.

The report is expected to follow a shared template, with the more organized structure from the previous year proposed as a reference. Drafts should be reviewed and validated, with version history retained in GitHub. Final ISSP and BCP work follows the workshops.

## Delivery risks noted

- **Unclear ownership and staffing:** Final roles and later workshop assignments are unresolved. Confirm them at the next meeting and record owners in the project plan.
- **Workshop 1 dependency:** Later analysis relies heavily on its scope and asset inventory. Protect capacity for this workshop and review its outputs before locking Workshop 2 staffing.
- **Coordination and version-control errors:** GitHub changes could overwrite work. Use branches, reviewed changes, and clear commit practices.
- **Availability:** Not all members may attend each meeting. Keep written decisions and use asynchronous updates for routine coordination.
- **Scope overload:** The supply-chain case includes multiple layers, AI, and workflows. Prioritize critical assets and processes and document exclusions.
